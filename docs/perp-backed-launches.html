Curveball / product brief

Perp-backed launches

Product summary for the team · design draft, 17 November 2026 · not approved to build yet

The idea in one paragraph

A creator launches a token that comes with a published, guaranteed minimum USDC redemption value — a “floor.” The same launch also lets the creator run one separate trading position on RISEx, up to 10× leverage, as a strategy for the token. Anyone who holds the token can burn it at any time and get the published USDC floor from a reserve. If the trading strategy wins, the realized profits can push the floor higher. If it loses or gets liquidated, only the trading money is at risk — the floor reserve is untouchable.

What “floor” does not mean: it is not a guaranteed sale price on the market, not protection against the token's market price going down, and not insurance against USDC, the chain, or the platform failing. The floor is specifically “how much USDC a holder can redeem from the reserve under the contract rules.” Market price can be above or below it.

This is a new launch type. Existing Curveball tokens and the normal WETH launch flow stay exactly as they are.

Why we need it

Current launches sell tokens, open trading, and lock the money raised into market liquidity — holders get no redeemable claim and no money-back promise. This design adds two things: a reserve that actually backs a published floor, and a perp position that gives the token a story and a chance to raise that floor over time.

The technical heart of it: the money must be split into three separate pots, because each pot has a different job and a different risk. This is the single most important design rule.

Pot of money Where it comes from What it may be used for
Floor reserve Creator's USDC deposit; later realized profits Holder redemptions and nothing else
Trading margin Separate creator USDC deposit One trading position; can be lost entirely
Market liquidity Launch capital and token inventory Normal trading (curve / pool); never counted as the floor

The economics in plain terms

A simple formula drives everything:

Floor per token = USDC in the reserve ÷ total tokens that can ever be in circulation.

Real example: a launch of 1 billion tokens promises a floor of $0.00001 per token. That needs $10,000 USDC in the reserve. A separate $1,000 trading margin is enough for roughly $10,000 of position at 10× leverage. If that position is liquidated, the $1,000 may be gone — the $10,000 redemption reserve is still fully there.

  • The floor is set when the launch activates and can only go up, and only once real USDC actually lands in the reserve.
  • Nobody counts “promised” money: no unrealized trading profit, no position equity, no pool reserves, no future deposits.
  • Every token that could eventually circulate counts against the floor — including unsold inventory and tokens set aside for the pool.
  • Redemptions shrink both the reserve and the token count together at the current floor; small rounding leftovers stay in the reserve.
  • The creator pays for both the reserve and the trading margin up front. Buying tokens on the curve does not fund either.

The product journey, step by step

  1. Prepare. Creator picks a RISEx market, long or short, the 10× target, the floor amount, and the trading margin. The launch form shows exactly how much USDC is needed, the position size, and honest examples of funding, liquidation, and whether the asset is even listed.
  2. Fund. The reserve receives the floor money; a separate account receives the trading margin. Neither comes from existing trading fees or treasury money.
  3. Open. The operator places the order and must verify an actual, filled position — a submitted order is not enough. A partial fill must meet a minimum exposure or the position is closed before launch.
  4. Activate. Trading opens only after the reserve is funded and the position is verified. If anything fails or times out, no backed market opens: the strategy is closed and uncommitted creator funds are returned.
  5. Trade. The market page shows the token price and a separate live “backing” panel. Holders can redeem at any time — even mid-launch, during an outage, or after a liquidation.
  6. Settle profits. The operator can close or reduce the position. A floor increase is recorded only after realized USDC is actually withdrawn and deposited into the reserve.
  7. Graduate. Normal trading liquidity can move into a pool as usual. The reserve and redemption right continue unchanged, and pooled tokens stay redeemable once a user owns them. Graduation never sends reserve or trading money into the pool.
  8. End state. If the position is liquidated, we say so plainly and keep redemptions open. Nothing silently opens a replacement position or uses reserve money to rescue it.

What we show in the product

  • Launch form: a distinct “backed launch” path with the floor, margin, worst-case loss, and liquidation sensitivity shown before committing. The ordinary launch stays simple.
  • Market card: a “backed” badge only on verified, activated markets, with the current redeemable USDC value per token. Pending or liquidated positions get their own status — never hidden.
  • Market page: reserve balance and contract link; the floor and redeemable supply; the position's side, size, entry, current health, funding, and liquidation state; last successful update time; and separate buttons for normal trading vs. on-chain redemption.
  • Operator view: funding/order/activation state, balance checks between the API and the chain, stale-data and margin alerts, close/reduce controls, recovery from failed steps, and an audit log.

Worth saying for naming and marketing: “floor-backed launch with a perp strategy” describes the design more accurately than “perp-backed launch.” The position supplies the upside story; the reserve supplies the actual protection.

What is deliberately out of scope for version one

  • Making existing tokens retroactively “backed.”
  • Automatic re-leveraging after losses, automatic top-ups, or using the reserve as margin.
  • Promising trading profits, profit forecasts, or a guaranteed market price.
  • Community-funded pieces of the position, creator withdrawals from the floor, dynamic leverage, multiple positions, or per-market buybacks.
  • Counting an unfilled order, unrealized profit, or a wallet balance as backing.

What has to be true before we build it

  1. Market exists. The intended asset must actually be listed on RISEx with the order size and leverage we need. As of the draft date, there is no RISE/USDC pair — if “RISE perp” means the RISE token, that market is currently unavailable.
  2. We can control the position safely. Prove a dedicated account can be owned by our contract or smart wallet, with signer permissions and withdrawal destination locked down. Otherwise we document and accept operator custody for the trading leg.
  3. The math is safe. Simulate simultaneous selling and redeeming, supply changes, arbitrage, rounding, and graduation to prove the reserve can always cover every outstanding token's floor.
  4. It works end to end on testnet. Deposit, signer registration, margin setup, order, confirmed fill, close, withdraw, reconciliation — including partial fills and an aborted launch after a position was opened.
  5. Operations and assurance. Who holds emergency authority, how a signer gets revoked, what happens when RISEx is down, independent contract review, and the legal review needed before marketing a redeemable financial product.

The decisions we still need to make

Question Working recommendation
What does “backed” mean? An on-chain USDC redemption right per token, separate from the trading margin
Who funds it? Creator funds both the reserve and the margin up front
What position? One isolated 10× position on a currently supported market
Who gets profits? Realized net USDC goes into the reserve, raising the floor
What happens on a loss? Trading margin may go to zero; reserve and redemptions continue
Where does token trading happen? A new backed launch path, with pool liquidity after graduation
Can the operator touch the floor? No — reserve withdrawals are holder redemptions only

The one big unanswered question

Do we really promise an enforceable floor? If yes, this whole design — the reserve, the token accounting, the activation rules — is the core requirement. If the real intent is instead “a token treasury that takes a 10× trade and buys back tokens with the wins,” that is a different, simpler product, and it should be named and scoped as such — no floor claim at all. This choice governs how much we build.
